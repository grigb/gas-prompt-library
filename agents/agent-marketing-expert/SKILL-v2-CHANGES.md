## Why this reimagining exists

A real DC session used this skill's own guidance and still produced cold, institutional, infrastructure-first copy that buried the human why and who. The root cause was in the skill: it leaned hard on EXTERNAL research and treated the project's own raw material as a minor principle, it implied "lead with the moment" without naming the trap, and its open-source guidance told every artifact to read like a standards body. v2 fixes those five gaps while preserving all of v1's craft depth.

This is v1 plus five fixes, cleanly integrated as elevated laws, new failure-modes, and one new diagnostic. It is not a rewrite. Every v1 mode, protocol, ledger, checklist item, and knowledge-base format is intact.

## The five changes

### 1. Soul-Grounding Law (new top-level section)

What changed: added "The Grounding Law: Soul Before Distillation" as a most-important behavioral rule in copywriter and architect modes, co-equal with dual advocacy. It mandates mining the project's OWN raw body of work (founder voice, origin story, manifestos, needs analysis) and lifting real language before writing. It introduces the "source shelf" concept: a curated map of where the raw passion and needs live, distinct from the distilled style guide. Reinforced in research principle 9 (rewritten from "Project Documents Are Source Material" into "Ground in the Project's Own Soul First"), Phase C1 GROUND, Phase C2 DRAFT, the quality checklist (new item 4), hard constraint 20, an anti-pattern, and a new persisted index (CONTEXT-BRIEF-INDEX.md).

Why: distillations and external benchmarks are necessary but not sufficient. The emotional core comes from the real material. v1 buried this as principle 9 of 9.

Risk and tradeoff: adds a grounding step before drafting, so a copy task costs more up front when a project has a deep body of work. Mitigation is built in: for projects with no source shelf, the agent builds a lightweight one as it goes, so the cost is paid once and inherited. Low risk; this is the fix most directly tied to the failure that motivated the work.

### 2. The Ordering Law (new top-level section)

What changed: added "The Ordering Law: Why Before What" mandating WHY, then WHO, then WHAT and HOW, then OFFER on every outward surface. It shows how the existing five-layer model (Recognition, Tension, Change, Proof, Action) maps onto the ordering, so the two frameworks agree rather than compete. Names the failure by name: "Category-first and infrastructure-first opening," added as failure-mode 11 in the detector, plus quality-checklist item 3, hard constraint 19, an anti-pattern, and steps in C1 and C2.

Why: v1 implied "lead with the moment" but never made the ordering explicit or warned against the specific trap of opening on what-kind-of-thing-this-is or the org's structure.

Risk and tradeoff: the ordering is stated as a law with an explicit escape hatch (project mandate, or a late-funnel page whose job is the offer), so it will not wrongly force a conversion page to hide its ask. Low risk. The main tradeoff is prescriptiveness; the escape hatch and the "a category can be true and still be the wrong opening" framing keep it from becoming dogma.

### 3. Register reconciliation (rewritten domain guidance)

What changed: rewrote the "Open Standards / Open Source Infrastructure" domain guidance to split two cases that v1 conflated. Deep-technical standards projects use the standards-body register (W3C, IETF, Linux Foundation) on spec, how-it-works, standards-fit, and roadmap pages. Mission-driven open-source projects lead their front door and emotional core with the human movement and reserve that register for technical and evaluator pages only. Added a "Register-selection rule" (match the register to the page's audience, not the project's self-image), quality-checklist item 14, hard constraint 22, and an anti-pattern.

Why: v1 said public artifacts "should look like standards-body work, not startup marketing." That is right for evaluator and spec pages and actively wrong for the front door of a mission-driven project. It is part of why the copy came out cold.

Risk and tradeoff: this narrows, it does not delete, the standards-body guidance; the technical-page register survives intact, and the roadmap-copy craft section (which correctly targets evaluators) is unchanged. Low risk. The only judgment call the agent must now make is "is this page a front door or a technical page," which the audience-routing section already equips it to answer.

### 4. Context Inheritance (new subsection plus startup wiring)

What changed: added "Context inheritance (load before you write)" to the Project Context section, and wired it through the startup actions (new step 1), the Initialization Sequence (Step 3 rewritten), the source hierarchy (context brief inserted at position 2, source shelf at 7), Phase C1, hard constraint 21, and a persisted CONTEXT-BRIEF-INDEX.md format and post-session step. It mandates loading the project context brief, active mandates, and source shelf at startup, not just the style guide, and states that a dispatching orchestrator or steward MUST pass or point to this context, and the agent must load it before writing.

Why: your explicit concern was that dispatched agents start from zero and re-introduce drifts already fixed. A style guide alone does not carry accumulated positioning decisions, current mandates, guardrails, and where the soul lives.

Risk and tradeoff: introduces a dependency on a document that many projects will not have. Handled by making it conditional (load "if one exists") and by telling the steward or lead to create or grow one over time. The DC brief you supplied is a working example of exactly this document, so the pattern is proven in the field, not theoretical. Low risk.

### 5. The Opening Audit (new diagnostic)

What changed: added "The Opening Audit" to Content Architecture as an architect-mode and critic-mode diagnostic, with its own ledger. It sweeps every outward artifact, classifies how each one OPENS (leads with why/who, with what/how, or with the offer), names and protects the pages already doing it right, traces the drift to its upstream source (a codified category-first talk-track, a page template, an email pattern, an entity-narrative spine), and produces a prioritized fix-list where fixing the upstream template cascades. Referenced from failure-mode 11 and the register rule.

Why: a site-wide opening drift is usually one codified root cause, not many independent mistakes. v1 had no reusable way to find it or to protect the pages that were already right.

Risk and tradeoff: adds a diagnostic the agent must know when to run; it is scoped to "when inheriting an existing site or a multi-surface set," so it will not fire on a single fresh hero. Low risk, high leverage. This is the change most likely to save time on real cleanup jobs.
